# Proto Personas

## How these were built

These personas are synthesized from a series of long-form interviews conducted across the 2 months (the TCI series: six conversations with artists, researchers, writers, and animators working across creative communities). The interviews were not originally collected for Vínculo. They were collected because something in each conversation felt worth keeping. When this project shifted direction mid-semester, those conversations turned out to describe, almost in unison, the gap that Vínculo addresses.

Five themes emerged across the six interviews:

- **Community as infrastructure, not aesthetic** — collaboration is the ground, not the decoration
- **The lone-author myth** — every interviewee, in some register, pushed back against the idea that work comes from a single mind
- **Credit and the unseen** — the people who shape work often disappear from the record
- **Slowness and intentionality** — recurring across every conversation, in different vocabularies
- **Tactile language for connection** — webs, maps, roots, threads, wampum, composts — never "platforms" or "networks"

These themes shaped two personas. Each persona corresponds to a way of being in the network: not a demographic profile, but a behavioral and philosophical orientation.

---

## Persona 1 — The Observer

**Description**

A person who participates in creative or intellectual work but does not actively perform themselves on social platforms. They are present in communities through reading, watching, contributing, and showing up — not through posting.

**Anchored in**

The conversations with Amelia Winger-Bearskin (media artist, professor) and César Fieiras Ceide (researcher in AI and media). Both articulate a relationship to community that is rooted in values and care, and a wariness of platforms that mistake visibility for participation.

> *"It looks like slowing down when you want to go faster. Asking who isn't in the room before you start building."* — Amelia

> *"Cognitive debt is everything that the journalist stops doing and therefore often forgets to do by making this cognitive delegation."* — César

**Characteristics**

- Rarely posts publicly; engages through participation rather than performance
- Critical of platforms that reward visibility over contribution
- Values intentionality, slowness, and structural transparency
- Would ask *whose values are encoded in this?* before adopting a tool

**Pain points**

- Existing platforms reward visibility; their actual work and contributions remain illegible
- Feels present in communities but not structurally represented
- Distrusts tools that don't live their own claims

**Needs**

- A way to appear in a network without having to construct a public-facing identity
- A system that reflects participation rather than online activity
- Recognition through contribution, not performance

**Relevance to Vínculo**

The Observer is already in the network through the work they have done. Vínculo lets them be visible without requiring them to post. They are included because they participated, not because they performed.

## Persona 2 — The Contributor

**Description**

A person who actively collaborates across projects and roles within creative or intellectual communities. They name people. They credit collaborators. They work in webs, not solo.

**Anchored in**

The conversations with Mindy Seu (designer, writer, archivist), Matthew Prebeg (writer, digital culture researcher), Joseph Brett (claymation animator, co-founder of Jackdaw Films), and Roopa Vasudevan (media artist, researcher). Each of them, in different ways, has already invented a small-scale version of what Vínculo is trying to make general.

> *"No artwork is ever coming from a single perspective or a single mind or a single pair of hands."* — Roopa

> *"Ideas percolate through friends, lectures, therapy, students."* — Mindy

> *"My professional background is in academic research, so I'm very much the researcher guy who wants to gather all the citations, connect them with strings, make a little map of how it all comes together."* — Matt

> *"Like the jackdaws: we improvise, we make do and mend, we cruise around nicking from skips, and we borrow from friends."* — Joseph

**Characteristics**

- Works across multiple projects with different collaborators and varying roles
- Credits people by name, in public, as part of the practice
- Tracks relationships through shared work, not platforms
- Reaches for tactile metaphors, webs, maps, roots, composts, to describe how they work

**Pain points**

- Credit is uneven; one name surfaces while collaborators disappear
- Collaboration history is fragmented across Substack, Are.na, Instagram captions, and DMs
- No existing system represents the full structure of shared work

**Needs**

- A way to credit collaborators accurately and visibly
- A system that reflects roles and the actual web of contribution
- A visible structure of how work connects people over time

**Relevance to Vínculo**

The Contributor is already doing what Vínculo is for — they have just been carrying the work in patches across other tools. Vínculo gives them a place where the web is the thing itself.

---

## Key insight

Both personas are already part of a network. That network already exists. It just is not visible in existing systems.

Vínculo does not construct connections, it reveals them through shared participation in real projects and organizations. The Observer is included because they showed up. The Contributor is included because they named the people who showed up with them.

The lone author is a fiction the internet sold us, and the people who actually make things have always known it.
